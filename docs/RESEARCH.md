# Research Agenda

Eidolve is not only a software implementation problem. Building a useful Universe requires advances in information systems, knowledge representation, search, memory, human–AI interaction, autonomous research, and the organization of long-lived intelligent work.

The research program is organized around a single question:

> How can information, knowledge, artifacts, experience, research, systems, and intelligence form one persistent and continuously developing Universe?

## Universal representation without flattening

The Universe must connect heterogeneous objects without forcing them into one impoverished format. Papers, code, data, images, models, experiments, conversations, concepts, claims, people, tools, and projects have different internal structures and uses.

The problem is to preserve those differences while giving them stable identity, shared relations, discoverability, history, and interoperability.

## Continuity across boundaries

Current systems repeatedly lose knowledge at the boundaries between applications, machines, organizations, models, projects, users, and sessions.

Eidolve must investigate how a world can remain coherent while being distributed across repositories, databases, storage systems, search indexes, servers, and local environments. Synchronization should preserve identity, lineage, relations, and meaning rather than merely copy bytes.

## Search for understanding and research

Search in Eidolve must go beyond lexical match or vector similarity. It should find what is useful for the present question by combining content, structure, relations, evidence, history, relevance, and research context.

A central research problem is how to rank and organize heterogeneous material for different people, models, agents, and tasks without reducing the Universe to one fixed retrieval method.

## From information to shared knowledge

Files and search results do not automatically become understanding. The Universe must support the formation of durable knowledge from artifacts, evidence, research, criticism, synthesis, and revision.

This includes the relationship among original sources, claims, supporting and conflicting evidence, conceptual structure, Wiki-like synthesis, and the history of how understanding changes.

## Long-term memory and experience

Agent memory should not be an isolated store of summaries. Experience must remain connected to the projects, decisions, evidence, environments, tools, failures, and outcomes that gave it meaning.

Eidolve must study how experience becomes useful long-term memory, how repeated work becomes transferable knowledge, how feedback becomes reusable strategy, and how validated capability can persist across models, agents, versions, and systems.

## Context for intelligence

A model or agent can only attend to a small part of the Universe at one time. The system must construct working context that is appropriate to the present objective, the participating intelligence, and the available resources.

The research challenge is not merely retrieving more information, but selecting and organizing the part of the Universe that best supports understanding, reasoning, research, and decision-making while preserving paths back to the larger world.

## Autonomous research and discovery

The Universe should support the complete life of research: problems, background, literature, hypotheses, methods, code, experiments, evidence, failures, results, criticism, decisions, and open questions.

It should allow agents and humans to recover prior work, detect unresolved contradictions, transfer methods between domains, generate meaningful questions, design and compare experiments, evaluate progress, and accumulate research capability over long periods.

## Intelligence and system co-development

Eidolve should serve not only inference, but also learning, evaluation, training, and system improvement. Historical research, feedback, task outcomes, environments, tools, and model behavior can become material for better datasets, evaluations, strategies, agents, harnesses, and models.

The long-term goal is a productive relationship in which models and agents improve their use of the Universe, while their research and experience improve the Universe itself.

## Evaluation

Eidolve should be judged by whether it improves real work, not by the size of its graph, index, database, or file collection.

The decisive questions are whether it enables better continuity, discovery, understanding, reuse, reasoning, decisions, research, collaboration, transfer of experience, and long-term development across people and intelligent systems.
