# Foundations

Eidolve does not begin from a single database, model, framework, or product category. It combines several mature ideas into one coherent Universe.

These foundations are not independent modules placed beside one another. Each contributes a different property that the whole system requires.

## Git: history, evolution, and contribution

Git demonstrates that complex work can remain distributed, versioned, branchable, mergeable, and attributable without losing its history.

Eidolve carries this principle beyond source code. Knowledge, research, system design, memory, interpretation, and shared understanding also change over time. Their evolution should be visible. Alternative directions should be able to coexist. Contributions from people and agents should be reviewable and mergeable. Earlier states should remain recoverable.

Git is therefore a foundational model for how the Universe evolves, not a claim that every file, dataset, model, or event must be stored directly in Git.

## Knowledge graph: relations and structure

A world cannot be represented by folders alone. The same object may belong to many projects, depend on many ideas, support one claim, contradict another, originate from one experiment, and be reused by several agents.

The knowledge graph provides the general relational structure of the Universe. It connects artifacts, concepts, claims, evidence, people, agents, models, tools, projects, events, and histories without forcing them into a single hierarchy.

Trees remain useful for navigation, decomposition, and local organization, but they are views of a more general graph. A single tree cannot express the full structure of research or knowledge.

## Wiki: cumulative synthesis

Search results and raw files are not yet shared understanding. A Wiki turns dispersed material into continuously revised accounts of what is known, why it matters, how it is connected, where evidence comes from, and what remains unresolved.

Eidolve adopts the essential Wiki principles of linked pages, collaborative revision, readable synthesis, transparent history, and cumulative knowledge. A Universe must preserve original material while also allowing people and agents to build clear, canonical, evolving explanations above it.

The Wiki is not a second copy of the world. Its pages are syntheses connected back to the underlying artifacts, relations, evidence, research, and history.

## Search: discovery, relevance, and scale

A Universe becomes useful only when the right part of it can be found without already knowing where it is stored.

Eidolve draws from the core ideas of large-scale search: continuous discovery, indexing, query understanding, relevance, ranking, linking, and access across heterogeneous information. Search must operate over more than text. It must be able to use files, metadata, relations, histories, projects, evidence, concepts, and the needs of the current task.

The goal is not merely to return items containing similar words. It is to identify what is most useful for understanding, reasoning, research, and decision-making.

## Files and native artifacts: the substance of the world

The Universe must preserve information in its native and useful forms: documents, PDFs, Markdown, LaTeX, code repositories, JSON, tables, images, presentations, datasets, model artifacts, experiment outputs, logs, conversations, and future formats.

Structured representations should enrich these artifacts, not replace them. A knowledge graph without its source materials is incomplete. A Wiki without evidence becomes detached. Search without original content becomes shallow. Memory without the experience it summarizes becomes unreliable.

Files, data, and artifacts are therefore first-class parts of the Universe rather than attachments to a knowledge layer.

## Intelligence and research: use changes the world

Eidolve is not built only to preserve and retrieve information. The Universe must directly support cognition, reasoning, research, judgment, and action.

People, models, and agents should be able to enter the same world, discover what is relevant, understand prior work, compare evidence, develop hypotheses, run research, make decisions, create new artifacts, revise knowledge, and leave the Universe richer than they found it.

Research is therefore intrinsic to the architecture. Questions, methods, experiments, failures, results, criticism, and decisions are not secondary records. They are part of the world from which future intelligence learns and acts.

## The unity

The foundations can be summarized without reducing the project to a checklist:

- Git gives the Universe history and evolution.
- The graph gives it relations and structure.
- The Wiki gives it synthesis and shared understanding.
- Search gives it discovery and relevance.
- Native artifacts give it substance.
- Intelligence and research give it purpose and continued development.

Eidolve is the attempt to make these properties belong to one Universe.
