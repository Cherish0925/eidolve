# Contributing to Eidolve

Contributions should make the Universe more accurate, connected, intelligible, reusable, or capable of supporting research and intelligent work.

Read the [Research and Engineering Standard](docs/RESEARCH_AND_ENGINEERING_STANDARD.md) before contributing.

A substantive contribution should state, where applicable:

- the objective and why it matters;
- the objects, assumptions, constraints, and dependencies involved;
- the source artifacts, data, code, prior work, and environment;
- the method and the result it produced;
- the test, experiment, computation, proof, comparison, or external outcome used to assess it;
- failures, counterexamples, limits, and alternative explanations that affect the conclusion;
- which part of Eidolve or Universe changes, and what becomes possible or better as a result.

New abstractions, schemas, modules, documents, and terminology should be introduced only when they improve implementation, correctness, search, reasoning, transfer, verification, or discovery. Reorganization alone is not progress.

Changes to models, harnesses, retrieval, memory, tools, prompts, environments, or evaluations should identify the changed component and compare it under controlled conditions whenever practical. Benchmark gains should include contamination checks, regressions, cost, reliability, and relevance to real work.

Preserve original artifacts and provenance. Do not replace source material with detached summaries. Keep facts, derived conclusions, hypotheses, assumptions, estimates, and speculation distinguishable.

Prefer the smallest change that resolves the actual problem without narrowing the long-term scope of Eidolve.
